Overview

Mentcare is built in blocks by five teams and assembled into one system at the end of the semester. Team 4 builds the Admissions and Outreach block: everything that happens between a doctor deciding a patient needs specialist mental health care and that patient attending their first clinic appointment, and the outreach that keeps patients and their families in contact with the service afterwards.

In Mentcare, every patient is referred, either by their own doctor, by an Accident and Emergency doctor, or by a hospital doctor after treatment. The block receives these referrals, lets a clinician triage them, and turns accepted referrals into patient records with a booked first appointment. Because patients with mental health problems often miss appointments or lose contact, the block also sends letters and text reminders, follows up missed appointments automatically, and runs group sessions and information evenings for carers.

The block does not hold consultations, prescriptions, or risk assessments; those belong to other blocks. Screens in this guide are mockups of the planned product; names and figures are sample data. Numbered markers on each screen match the steps below it.

Referrers

GPs, A&E doctors and hospital doctors send referrals into the service.

1. Send a referral

Referrer (GP, A&E or hospital doctor), AO-01, AO-03; Mentcare 1.1, 3.1.1, 3.1.9

Every Mentcare patient arrives through a referral from their own doctor, an Accident and Emergency doctor, or a hospital doctor after a course of treatment. Referrers sign in with their health authority account and complete one form. They can attach the referral letter and any supporting documents, and they indicate how urgent the referral is and whether there are safety concerns.

Figure 1. Send a referral.

  1. Enter the patient's details. If the patient does not have or know their NHS number, leave it empty; Mentcare will generate a unique patient ID when the referral is accepted.
  2. Describe the reason for referral: symptoms, history, and treatment tried so far.
  3. Choose the urgency (Routine, Soon, or Urgent) and indicate any known risk of harm. Both are required and are used to prioritise the referral.
  4. Attach the referral letter and any recent reports.
  5. Record which ways the patient has agreed to be contacted. Outreach messages only use the channels ticked here.
  6. Select Send referral. Any missing or invalid field is outlined in red with an explanation. Once sent, the referral appears under My referrals with the status Received, and the referrer is told when a decision is made.

3. Triage a referral and decide

Triage clinician, AO-02, AO-03, AO-04; Mentcare 2.7, 3.1.12

The triage clinician reads the referral and documents, checks whether the patient already has a Mentcare record, sets the priority, and makes a decision: accept, ask the referrer for more information, or redirect to a more suitable service. The referrer is informed of the decision automatically.

Figure 3. Triage a referral and decide.

  1. Read the reason for referral, the referrer's urgency and risk assessment, and the attached letter.
  2. Check the existing-records match. Mentcare searches by name, date of birth, and NHS number. If the patient was seen before, select Same person to reopen their record instead of creating a duplicate; here the dates of birth differ, so it is a different person.
  3. Set the priority. It can differ from the urgency the referrer gave.
  4. Choose a decision. For Accept, choose the clinic and responsible consultant.
  5. Write a message to the referrer and select Save decision. The referrer is notified and can see the new status under My referrals.

4. Admit the patient: create the record and first appointment

Intake coordinator, AO-04, AO-05; Mentcare 2.3.2, 3.1.1, 3.1.5, 3.3.1

Once a referral is accepted, the intake coordinator admits the patient. The new patient record is filled in from the referral, so nothing is typed twice. The first appointment is booked in the APPOINTMENTS system, and the record is handed to the patient records block, where clinical staff take over.

Figure 4. Admit the patient: create the record and first appointment.

  1. Check the patient record. It is filled in from the referral; correct anything that has changed. If the patient has no NHS number, a unique Mentcare ID is generated.
  2. Choose a first appointment from the free slots in the APPOINTMENTS system for the chosen clinic and consultant, and select Book selected slot.
  3. Select Admit patient. The record is sent to the patient records block, the appointment is booked, the welcome letter and reminder sequence start, and the referrer is told. The ticks show each step succeeded; any step that fails is shown in red with a Retry button.

  1. Choose the patient. Their carers are listed only if the patient has agreed they may be contacted.

6. Set up automatic reminders and follow-up

Intake coordinator, AO-07; Mentcare 1.1, 3.3.3, 5.8

Many patients miss appointments, so Mentcare sends reminders automatically and follows up when an appointment is missed. Each reminder sequence is a timed list of steps. A sequence stops by itself when the patient rebooks, is discharged, or is detained in hospital.

Figure 6. Set up automatic reminders and follow-up.

  1. Choose a sequence from the list, or create a new one.
  2. Check what starts the sequence, for example an appointment marked Did not attend.
  3. Each step has a delay, a channel (text, letter, email, or a task for staff), and a message. Only channels the patient consented to are used; steps for other channels are skipped.
  4. Check the stop conditions, so patients who have rebooked or are in hospital are not contacted.
  5. Select Save sequence and set it to Active. The list shows how many patients are currently in each sequence.
